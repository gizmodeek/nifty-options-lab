7. Learn the terms

Delta
How much the position's value changes for a 1-point rise in spot. Calls are positive, puts negative. Delta near ±0.5 roughly means a 50% chance of finishing in the money.
Gamma
How fast delta changes as spot moves. High near expiry and at-the-money. Long options have positive gamma; sellers have negative gamma and face sharper losses on big moves.
Theta
Value lost per day from time decay, all else equal. Option buyers pay theta; option sellers earn it. Decay speeds up as expiry nears.
Vega
Change in value for a 1-percentage-point change in IV. Long options gain when IV rises; short options lose.
Intrinsic and time value
Intrinsic value is what an option would pay right now if exercised. The rest of the premium is time value, which decays to zero at expiry.
Breakeven
The spot price at expiry where the strategy's profit is exactly zero after premiums.
Stop loss and position sizing
A stop loss is a level where you exit to cap a loss. Position sizing limits how much capital is at risk in one trade. Most education guides suggest risking a small fixed percentage of capital per trade. Defined-risk strategies (spreads, iron condors) make the maximum loss known before entry; naked sells do not.
